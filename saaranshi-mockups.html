Comparing patient vs doctor on cost barriers
Scope · 12 patient interviews + 3 doctor interviews
What did patients say about treatment cost, and how does it differ from how doctors describe the same issue?
C
Claude · with corpus context

The most striking pattern is a difference in framing, not just emphasis. Patients describe cost in terms of what they had to give up to afford treatment — selling jewelry, taking loans, asking relatives. Doctors talk about cost as a workflow problem: navigating Arogyasri paperwork, treatment delays from incomplete coverage.

Three specific patterns:

1. Patients foreground the family economy. Eight of twelve patient interviews mention selling assets or borrowing. Most don't quantify it — the framing is moral and emotional, not financial.

"నా బంగారం అమ్మాను… అత్తగారిది కూడా. కానీ ఒక మాట కూడా అనలేదు ఆవిడ."
P-007 · 23:47 · Stage III, chemo

2. Doctors describe systemic friction, not patient distress. They mention Arogyasri but emphasize delays in approval, not the human toll.

"Arogyasri covers a lot, but the approval cycle adds 2–3 weeks. For a Stage III patient, that's a real problem we can't solve."
D-003 · 11:08 · Surgical onco, MNJ

3. The gap is the finding. Patients describe cost as a moral economy of family sacrifice; doctors describe it as a process bottleneck. Neither side is wrong — they're seeing the same thing from incommensurable angles. This may be worth its own subsection in your findings.
